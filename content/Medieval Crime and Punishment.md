#History/Crime-and-Punishment 
# Medieval 
Medieval ranges from 1000-1450. It is split into 3 time periods: Anglo Saxons (1000-1066), Normal (1066-1155), Later Medieval (1155-1450).
# Crimes
#### Anglo-Saxon
1. **Theft** was the most common crime. Other common crimes include **Assault, Murder, Arson**.
2. **Treason** existed but was unlikely as people kept to their own community and weren't interested in those of authority.
3. **Moral Crimes** - The church defined some behaviour as criminal such as blasphemy, gambling, adultery.
#### Norman
1. **Forest Laws Introduced** (Poaching). William took control of the woodland and only people with a *Royal License could hunt there.*
2. **Murdrum**. Murdering a Norman was more serious than killing an Englishman (an attempt to protect Normans as English were unhappy with being invaded). Your whole village could be found guilty and *fined*.
3. **Outlaws**. It was made illegal to flee your village
4. **Treason**. William faced opposition from the English so treason became a more recognised crime.
#### Later Medieval
1. **Hersey Introduced**. The Lollards started to disagree with the church so this law was introduced making it illegal.
2. **High Treason Introduced (1351)**. Specifically plot to kill against the monarch after people wanted more rights after *Black Plague*.
# Law Enforcement
#### Anglo-Saxon
1. **Hue & Cry**
2. **Tithings**
3. **Reeve**. Respected villager *chosen by other villagers*. His job was to take charge of suspects and bring them to trial.
4. **King's Shire Reeve**. *Chosen by locals*. His job was to make sure the *King's code of law* was being carried out.
5. **Trial by Jury**. Suspect would stand in front of *other villagers* and they would hear evidence and decide if they were guilty.
6. **Trial by Ordeal**. Church would decide. Hand was burned/wounded, depending on if it healed you would be guilty or not.
7. **Oath of Innocence**. The suspect could swear that they were innocent. If the suspect was respected this could be enough for the jury.
8. **Manorial Court**. For small Local Crimes
9. **King's Court**. Where *Nobles* would be tried
#### Norman
*Hue and Cry, Tithings were kept as the Normans didn't have enough people to effectively police community*
1. **Trial by Combat**. 
2. **Castles and Cathedrals**. They were built as a symbol of Norman power. They were also places for prisoners to wait trial.
3. **Church Sanctuary**. Anyone could get 40 days protection from the church.
4. **King's Court 1154**. Change so that lower class people could be tried if crimes were committed against Normans
5. **Church Courts Created**
#### Later Medieval
1. **Trial by Ordeal Abolished 1215**
2. **Reeve replace with Parish Constable**. They were local villagers who volunteered but got power over other villagers.
3. **Coroners**. Investigated crimes, appointed by King.
4. **County Sherriff**. New name for *King's Shire Reeve*.
5. **Justices of Peace (After Black Plague)**. Made sure that people didn't break the law.
6. **Benefit of Clergy**. If you could read a bible verse then you could be pardoned.
7. **Buying a Pardon from The King**

# Punishment
#### Anglo-Saxon
1. **Wergild**. Fine *given to victim*
2. **Stocks and Pillories**. Rotten food or rocks thrown.
3. **Mutilation**. Stealing multiple times could get your hand cut off. Lies could get your tongue cut off
#### Norman
1. **Wergild**. Fine *changed to go to crown*
2. **Murdrum Fine**. Whole community charged fine if a Norman was killed.
3. **Mutilation**. Breaking forest laws would get your 2 bow fingers cut off.
4. **Higher Use of Capital and Corporal Punishment**. The Normans needed higher levels of deterrence to stop the crime targeted at them.

#### Later Medieval
1. **Hung Drawn and Quartered**. Semi-strangled then stomach cut open and intestines drawn out. Their limbs would be transported to different parts of the country to serve as a warning.

# Who Had The Biggest Role
In the *Anglo-Saxon* time, ***Community*** had the biggest influence over everything. ***The King*** had a little with *King's Code* and ***The Church*** with *moral crimes* but not much overall.
In the *Norman Times and Later Medieval*. **The King*** gained more influence as he now appointed people, *The King's Mund* was more definitive. Punishment more a deterrent against disobeying the King rather than community based. However **The Community** still had lot's of influence on *law enforcement* with *Hue & Cry and Tithings* as the Normans didn't have enough men to properly enforce law.

Overall in Anglo-Saxon times the community has the most influence over law enforcement and punishment with he king having some control of crimes. However form the Normans onwards the king took more and more control of crimes, punishment and law enforcement however the community still had a major role in law enforcement.
## Role of King
1. **King's Code of Law**. Less serious crimes left to interpretation
2. **King's Mund**
3. **King's Court**
4. **Appointed People:** *Reeve, King's Shire Reeve, Coroners, Justice of Peace (From Norman time onwards)*
5. **Buy a Pardon From King**
6. **Wergild**. *From Norman onward went to King*

## Role of Community
1. **Hue and Cry**
2. **Tithings**
3. **Reeve and King's Shire Reeves**. *Changed by Norman times*
4. **Jury**
5. **Oath of Innocence**
6. **Wergild**. *Anglo-Saxon Time went to victim*
7. **Stocks and Pillories**. *Based on humiliation in front of community*

## Role of Church
1. **Church Courts**
2. **Trial by Ordeal**
3. **Sanctuary**
4. **Benefit of Clergy**
5. **Moral Crimes**
