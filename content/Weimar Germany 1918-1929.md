# Weimar Constitution

1. President
	1. Elected every 7 years.
	2. Played little part in day-to-day politics.
	3. Chose *Chancellor* and *Cabinet*.
2. Government 
	1. Chancellor
		1. Head of government.
		2. Chose all government ministers.
	2. Cabinet
		1. Consists of the most important ministers.
		2. Worked closely with *Chancellor*.
		3. Main decision making body of government.
3. Parliament 
	- *All laws have to pass through both houses.*
	1. Reichstag
		1. Proportional Voting
		2. Controlled Taxation
		3. Directly elected by people at least once every 4 years.
	2. Reichsrat
		1. Elected every 4 years.
		2. Represented the regions of Germany.
		3. Each region sent a number of representatives proportional to their population.
## Strengths
1. Women were able to vote.
2. Proportional voting meant that smaller parties were given a fair share.
3. Some powers such as police, courts and schools were kept to local government.
4. Reichsrat could delay laws unless Reichstag has 2/3 majority.
## Weaknesses
1. 29 Different parties with proportional voting. No party had a majority so it was difficult to pass any laws.
2. Coalitions often formed.
3. Many radical parties managed to get some seats.
4. Abuse of ***Article 48***
## Article 48
Article 48 allowed the chancellor to ask the president to pass an "emergency" law without the use of the parliament.

# Treaty of Versailles
1. The treaty was a *diktat* treaty so Germany had no say in any of the terms.
2. Germany had to accept all responsibility for the war.
3. Germany would have to pay £6.6b in reparations.
4. Germany would lose all colonies. Would be looked after by other powers.
5. Army was restricted: 100k men, 6 battleships, 12 destroyers, 12 torpedo boats, **NO** air force.
6. Rhineland was to be demilitarised and allied troops would remain there until 1930.

# Challenges to The Weimar Republic
## Dolchstoss
This was a theory that, during the war, Germany was stabbed in the back by Jews, socialists and communists.
## Spartacist Revolt (Jan 1919)
1. Lead by (left wing): Rosa Luxembourg, Karl Liebknecht.
2. Took advantage of a strike and protest in Berlin where 100,000 workers went on strike. They took over the government's newspaper and telegraph officers.
3. Ebert used *Reichswehr* (the army) to organise the demobilised soldiers into *Freikorps* units. The unarmed workers were not a match for the armed Freikorps.
4. By *Jan 13th*, the Freikorps has won. By *Jan 16th* Luxembourg and Liebknecht were captured and killed
## Kapp Putsch (March 1920)
1. Lead by (right wing): Wolfgang Kapp
2. They wanted return of Kaiser Wilhelm II
3. 5000 men marched into Berlin armed. They declared a new government and invited the Kaiser to return.
4. Ebert sent the *Reichswehr* wo fire upon the Freikorps but they refused and many joined the rebellion.
5. The workers went on strike so critical services stopped and the rebels had to surrender.
6. app was put in prison.

# Hyperinflation
## Causes
1. Germany is bankrupt and can't pay reparations.
2. France were upset as they needed the money to pay American debt.
3. *Dec 1922* Germany was unable to make the reparation payment.
4. *Jan 1923* France seize the Ruhrland and all coal plants there.
5. Germany ordered coal workers to strike.
6. Lack of resources produced.
7. Money printed to pay workers so they continued to strike.
8. Over printing led to hyperinflation.
## Consequences
1. Poor people got poorer.
2. Prices went up.
3. Rich paid of debts in devalued currency.
4. People turned to bartering.

# Stressmann
*Gustav Stressmann* was leader of the *people's party* and foreign secretary in 1923. He wanted to stabilise the German economy and save it from hyperinflation. He believes that if he does this people will believe in democracy and stop voting for extremist parties.
## League of Nations
1. Established in *Treaty of Versailles* in *1920*.
2. It's main aims were to:
	1. Stop wars through collective security and negotiation.
	2. Encourage disarmament.
	3. Improve working conditions and health.
3. By *1938* it had 58 members.
4. Could apply sanctions.

Germany joined in *Sept 1926*. 
1. This made Germany once again seen as a great power.
2. Allowed Germany to negotiate the *Young Plan*.
3. Allied troops left the *Rhineland* earlier then planned in ToV.
4. However not everyone liked it as it was linked to the ToV
