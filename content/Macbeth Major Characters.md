# Macbeth
## Summary  
At the beginning of the play, Macbeth is presented as a brave and loyal soldier. However, ambition quickly corrupts him as he chooses murder to achieve power. By the end, Macbeth becomes a tyrannical, isolated figure, numb to fear and guilt.

## Quotes
1. "For brave Macbeth—well he deserves that name." – said by Captain when praising Macbeth’s heroism in battle.  
2. "So foul and fair a day I have not seen." – said by Macbeth when he echoes the witches’ words, hinting at his connection to evil forces.  
3. "If chance will have me king, why, chance may crown me without my stir." – said by Macbeth when he contemplates destiny without action.  
4. "Is this a dagger which I see before me, the handle toward my hand?" – said by Macbeth when he hallucinates before killing Duncan.  
5. "O, full of scorpions is my mind, dear wife!" – said by Macbeth when he reveals his growing paranoia about Banquo.  
6. "I am in blood stepped in so far that should I wade no more, returning were as tedious as go o'er." – said by Macbeth when he realises he cannot go back from his violent path.  
7. "I'll fight till from my bones my flesh be hacked." – said by Macbeth when he embraces his doomed, reckless defiance at the end.

# Lady Macbeth
## Summary  
Lady Macbeth initially appears strong, ruthless, and more ambitious than her husband. However, after Duncan’s murder, guilt consumes her, and her strength unravels. By the end, she becomes mentally broken and takes her own life under the weight of guilt.

## Quotes
1. "Glamis, thou art; and Cawdor; and shalt be what thou art promised." – said by Lady Macbeth when she receives Macbeth’s letter and vows to help him.  
2. "Come, you spirits that tend on mortal thoughts, unsex me here." – said by Lady Macbeth when she calls on dark powers to strip away her compassion.  
3. "Look like the innocent flower, but be the serpent under’t." – said by Lady Macbeth when she advises Macbeth to hide his evil intentions.  
4. "Screw your courage to the sticking-place, and we’ll not fail." – said by Lady Macbeth when she urges Macbeth to kill Duncan.  
5. "A little water clears us of this deed." – said by Lady Macbeth when she minimizes the murder's consequences.  
6. "Nought’s had, all’s spent, where our desire is got without content." – said by Lady Macbeth when she admits that achieving their goals has brought no peace.  
7. "Out, damned spot! Out, I say!" – said by Lady Macbeth when she sleepwalks, revealing her overwhelming guilt.

# Witches
## Summary  
The witches embody chaos, evil, and fate. They use riddles and prophecies to manipulate Macbeth and set the tragic events in motion. Throughout the play, they remain mysterious and otherworldly, never showing remorse or emotion.

## Quotes
1. "Fair is foul, and foul is fair." – said by the Witches when they set the theme of moral confusion.  
2. "All hail, Macbeth! Hail to thee, Thane of Glamis! All hail, Macbeth! Hail to thee, Thane of Cawdor! All hail, Macbeth, that shalt be king hereafter!" – said by the Witches when they prophesize Macbeth’s rise to power.  
3. "Lesser than Macbeth, and greater." – said by the Witches when they deliver paradoxical prophecies about Banquo.  
4. "Something wicked this way comes." – said by the Second Witch when sensing Macbeth’s approach.  
5. "Double, double toil and trouble; fire burn and cauldron bubble." – said by the Witches when they brew a spell before Macbeth’s second visit.  
6. "Macbeth! Macbeth! Macbeth! Beware Macduff." – said by the First Apparition summoned by the Witches to warn Macbeth.  
7. "Show his eyes, and grieve his heart; come like shadows, so depart!" – said by the Witches when they show Macbeth a vision of Banquo’s descendants becoming kings.
