# Changes
## Religious Change 
The religion of the monarch was changing very often and each had their own idea of how Christianity and churches should be run often annoying a large group of people who believed that it should be run differently.

## Population Growth
During the 16th and 17th century there was a steady increase in population increasing demand for food and competition for jobs.
## Political
There was a great rebellion: *The Civil War (1642-1649)* in which Parliament fought and beat the monarchy ending in the execution of ***Charles I***. This led to a feeling of insecurity lasting decades.
## Economic
England was wealthier overall and some people became richer however many people still remained poor and were vulnerable to price rises due to bad harvests.
## Landowner's Attitudes
Landowners were becoming richer and had growing influence. They encouraged laws that defended their rights, power and property.
## Printing
After the printing press was invented in the 15th century the spread of ideas rapidly increased especially about *crime, witchcraft and vagabonds.*

# Vagrancy
***Vagrants*** were people who were homeless and without a job and roamed the streets begging for money. Many often committed crimes to survive.
There are 2 types of vagrant: *deserving poor* and *undeserving poor*. The deserving poor are those who **CAN'T*** work (e.g. they have a severe disability). Whereas the undeserving poor are those who ***REFUSE*** to work.
## Increases in Vagrancy
1. Henry VII closed down monasteries.
	- Poor people would have gone to then for help with food or medical problems.
	- The people who worked there lost jobs.
	- No option for help so people had to resort to begging.
2. There was a big rise in population.
	- There was more competition for jobs and not enough for everyone so some people has to resort to begging.
	- Shortage of Housing so rent went up and many went homeless.
3. Landowners put fences around their land to farm sheep instead of crops.
	- Less crops meant prices went up and people couldn't afford to buy food.
	- People lost jobs form the farm as less people are needed to farm sheep.
4. There were bad harvests.
	- Less supply meant that prices went up and many people couldn't afford food.
	- Had to beg or steal to survive.
5. High taxes for war.
	- Poor people still had to pay high taxes on the little money they earn
6. Wages were increasing much slower compared to food prices. 
## Fear of Vagrants
1. People were worried that vagrants would steal from them - *especially rich people*.
2. Printing press produced a lot of pamphlets which contained exaggerated ideas and spread the concept that vagrants were violent and dangerous. *Thomas Harman's book published in 1567 described 23 types of vagrants* 
3. The bible taught that good Christians should work hard so people believed that vagrants weren't good Christians and therefore didn't deserve help.
## Legality of Vagrancy
The rich were so concerned with vagrants that they made it into a crime.
1. 1531 - Made illegal to be a vagrant. Vagrants would be whipped and returned to their birthplace.
2. 1547 - 1st offence leads to **2 years slavery**, 2nd offence leads to **slavery for life** or **execution**.
3. 1572 - 1st offence leads to **ear burned with hot poker**, 2nd offence leads to **prison**, 3rd offence leads to **execution**. The first ***Poor Rate*** was created: people paid a tax to help *deserving poor*
4. 1576 - JP's had to provide materials to *able bodied poor* to carry out trade. If they continued vagrancy they would be set to a *house of correction* designed to **Punish** and **Employ** beggars.
5. 1593 - 1572 Act abolished, 1531 act reintroduced.
6. 1598 - repeat offenders sent to a *house of correction.*

# Gunpowder Plot 1605
*James I* was protestant and hated Catholics so a group of Catholics plotted to put barrels of gunpowder under parliament and blow them up when he was there. They were caught as one of the plotters *Francis Tresham* sent a letter to his brother in law *(Lord Monteagle)* who reported it to the spy-master *Robert Ceil*.The group was ultimately hung drawn and quartered. This harsh punishment acted as a deterrent to anyone else who wanted to plot against the king.
## Consequences
The plot furthers James' anti-Catholic feelings so it leads to the following.
1. King James wrote a book encouraging anti-Catholic feelings.
2. **Thanksgiving Act** bas Catholics from being legal professional or officers in the army.
3. In 1606 the **Popish Recusants Act** was introduced which forced Catholics to swear an oath of allegiance to the crown and forced them to take part in church services.
4. Catholics were banned from: voting, becoming MP's and owning land until 1829.

# Witchcraft and Trials
The key "features of a witch" are: *older, living alone, deformity, woman, unmarried, having a pet*.
## Legality of Witchcraft
1. In the 14th century if you were accused of being a witch you might have to *pay a fine* or *say a prayer.*
2. In 1542 a law was passed allowing "witches" to be tried in *crown court* and the *punishment* would be **death**.
3. From 1604 anyone suspected of contacting evil spirits could be hanged.
## Increases in Witchcraft Trials
### Poverty
#### Causes
1. Population increase.
2. Crop failure.
3. Men killed in battle.
4. Monasteries dissolved.
#### Consequences
1. Widows
2. More beggars.
##### Effects on Witchcraft Accusations
1. Beggar asks person for help.
2. Refusal to help out poor due to own situation.
3. Something happens to person.
4. Beggar accused of witchcraft.
### Fear of Witches
1. When Henry VII broke away form the Catholic church he got rid of a lot of superstitions and got rid of *crucifixes*. This made Catholics feel vulnerable.
2. The Protestant church preached that the devil could send demons to walk on earth.
3. James I publish **Daemonolgie*** in 1597. It describe the horrors of witchcraft an explained how to identify a witch and run a trial.
### Mathew Hopkins - Witchfinder General
1. He used methods such as *torture* and *sleep deprivation* to extract confessions.
2. He was paid £1 (equivalent to ~£200) per witch he caught.
3. He had Puritan beliefs about eliminating evil from society.
4. During Civil War:
	1. Judges away with war related cases.
	2. Local magistrates left in charge of local law taking matters into their own hands.
5. After Civil War:
	1. Executed king created feeling of insecurity, suspicion and distrust so people were more likely to believe that others were witches.
