#History/Elizabeth 
# What was England Like in The 16th Century
## Economic
England has good trade links through the Netherlands and it's exports get sold through Europe. However their main export *wool* was at a low prices due to a high supply.
England has no empire to get resources from.
They main job sector was *farm labouring*.
## Political
England are quite *politically weak* as they don't have any real allies and their long lasting relationship with *Spain* is unstable due to Elizabeth and King Phillip's conflicting religion.
Their main trading partner *The Netherlands* are controlled by *Spain*.
They are weak to a joint attack from *Scotland and France*.
Had a war with *France* which was started by *Henry VII*.
## Religion
England is *Protestant* under Elizabeth leading them to be very isolated as their neighbouring countries are *Catholic*. This also leaves them weak to a *crusade*.
There is a big religious divide in England with many people being *Catholic* whilst others are *Protestant* (*Catholics* were more prominent in *the north* whilst there were more *Protestants in the south*) and there is widespread disagreement between them on how the church should be run.

# Elizabeth's Challenges
## International Relations
1. She struggles to make foreign alliances due to her Protestantism.
2. England needs to expand their empire in order to not be weak.
## Gender
1. Female leaders were seen as inferior an they needed to marry a man.
2. There will be competition over who she should marry.
## Marriage and Children
1. She is expected to have children to secure the Tudor dynasty but childbirth is dangerous due to bad healthcare.
## Religion
1. Catholics do not recognise her as a legitimate queen due to her mother (*Anne Boleyn*) being a 2nd wife and Catholics did not see divorce as legitimate.
2. Protestants and Catholics don't get on dividing England
## Money
1. She needs money for an army to not be weak.
2. Due to a war with France started by Henry VII, England is bankrupt.

# Structure of Society
## Class
### Monarch
1. Most important and powerful person in society.
2. Government centres around them.
3. Divine right to rule chosen by God.
4. Chooses the Privy Council.
5. Can choose if England goes to war.
6. Can call and dismiss parliament.
7. Can issue proclamations (however only acts passes through parliament can be enforced).
8. Can give patronage (jobs and titles) to whoever they want.
### Nobility
1. The 50th wealthiest landowning families.
2. Monarch picks 19 of them to be on the Privy Council.
### Gentry
1. Rich landowners but not the wealthiest.
2. Numbers growing over the last 50 years.
### Wealthy Citizens (Merchants, Yeoman, Tenant Farmers)
1. Oversee the poor giving the money, food and clothes.
### Poor (Craftsmen, Labourers, Servants)
1. Biggest % of populations.
2. Don't own land.
3. Depend on common land for materials and food.

## Elizabethan Government
### Parliament
1. Pass laws and taxes.
2. Raise taxes.
3. Get opinion of gentry so queen can make more informed decisions.
4. Only called when queen needs it.
### Privy Council
1. Advises her in making decisions.
2. Consists of 19 men chosen by her.
3. Can't talk about religion or marriage.
### Secretaries of State
1. Most important role in Privy Council.
2. Decided by queen.
3. Sir William Cecil and later Sir Francis Walsingham.
### Queen's Court
1. Members of nobility advise and entertain the queen.
2. Could gain power and patronage 
### Local Government
#### Justices of The Peace
1. Enforce queen's policies throughout the land they own.
2. Stop crimes and disorder.
3. Sometimes judge in court.
4. Raise troops for war.
5. Collect taxes.
6. Keep peace and ensure fair trade.
#### Lord Lieutenants
1. Each county has one.
2. Make sure the queens wishes and polices are carried out.

# Leisure and Entertainment
## Theatre
At the beginning of the Elizabethan period people enjoyed mystery plays and they were often performed in market places and inn yards and became very rowdy.
However the Privy council banned mystery plays as they thought they promoted *Catholic* ideas and would cause religious tension so there was a rise in secular plays.
In 1576 the first purpose built theatre was made and in the 1580's may more were built.
### Features of Elizabethan Theatres
1. Only men and boys could act in plays.
2. Most people watched standing in the yard/pit they were known as groundlings.
3. Roof covered part of the stage but it was mostly open sky.
4. Lords sit behind actors to be seen by others to show their status.
5. Galleries - covered seats around the pit sat in by the rich.
