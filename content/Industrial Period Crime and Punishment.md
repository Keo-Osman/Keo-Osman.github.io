# Changes to Britain in The Industrial Period
1. Population. Went from 9.5m to 41.5m from 1650 to 1900.
	1. More opportunity for crime.
	2. Less jobs and food so people were more likely to resort to crime.
	3. Easier to get away with. Blend in more.
2. Main job sector moved from agriculture to workshops and factories.
	1. Less guilt in stealing from a big factory then neighbours farm.
	2. Difficult to catch people in factory.
3. By 1885 nearly all men had the right to vote. Governments focused on improvements for lower class.
	1. Better conditions for lower classes lead to less crime.
	2. Harsher punishments to deter protests.
4. By the 19th century harvests were consistent.
	1. Lower food costs meant people didn't need to steal.
5. Railways became a major form of travel.
	1. Easier to rob on busy railway.
	2. Harder to catch criminals.
6. Higher taxes collected to pay for reforms and improve living standards.
	1. Less crime as basic needs are met.
	2. Tax evasion was more appealing.
7. In 1880 it was legally mandated that children had to go to school until 13.
	1. Higher literacy rates meant more people got better jobs so they were less likely to commit crime. 
8. New ideas about human nature.
	1. Lower crime rates.

# Definition of Crime
## Highway Robbery
### Reason For Increase
1. More people travelling in their own coaches.
2. Rough roads where coaches had to slow down.
3. Highwaymen could hide and sell loot in a tavern.
4. After wars with France handguns were easier to obtain.
5. No police force, constables could not track across counties.
### Reason For Decrease
1. Road surfaces improved.
2. Justices of Peace refused licenses to taverns where highwaymen frequented.
3. Banking system became more capable, less cash.
4. Patrols set up.

## Poaching

1. *The 1723 Black Act* made hunting deer, hare or rabbits a *capital crime*. 
2. Poaching was widely tolerated as many did it to survive and had previously been allowed so it was seen as a right.
3. *Gamekeepers* would try and stop poaching and got rewards for convicting one but they were hated and many poachers used violence against them.
4. Only people who owned *£100* worth of land could hunt.
5. Poor people poached for food or to sell on the black market.
6. Anyone found armed or disguised in a hunting area were assumed to be poaching and could be executed.

# Tolpuddle Martyrs
1. The ***Tolpuddle Martyrs*** were a group of 6 labourers for a farm.
2. Common Land was now enclosed and owned by the rich.
3. In 1833 their wage of *9 shillings* (which was only enough to buy bread to feed the family and nothing else) was *cut to 8 shillings then 7.*
4. They formed a union and went on strike until they got 10 shillings a week.
5. They were then tried and sentenced for making a secret oath.
6. They were given transportation to Australia as a deterrent to prevent further unionisation and to allow the rich to push for further wage decreases.
7. There was widespread outcry and 25,000 people attended a meeting in London. 250,000 people signed a petition.
8. In *March 1833* they were given a pardon however it was 2 years before they got to return and a further 20 before the trade union movement began to pick up again.

# Law Enforcement
## Bow Street Runners
***Henry Fielding*** (magistrate and novelist) set up the 1st police force *in 1748* called to ***Bow Street Runners***.
They attended crime scenes used *"thief takers"* and *"principial officers"* and published descriptions of wanted criminals to find/detect criminals. They charged fees for their service and collected rewards from the victim. However *by 1785* they were paid by the government.
The introduced organised regular foot and horse patrols by paid constable along major roads. They understood the importance of sharing information with other law enforcement.

## Metropolitan Police Force 1829
*Links*: [[Whitechapel#Police|Whitechapel Policing]]
### Reasons for Introduction
1. Widespread belief that violent crime was on the rise.
2. High food prices and unemployment lead to major protests *in 1815* which the government felt out of control for.
3. Taxes were already at high levels due to war with France after it ended the extra tax money was used to fund a police force.
4. Sir Robert Peel *(Home Secretary 1822)* used statistics to paint a picture of rising crime.
### Development 
1. *1829 -* Set up.
2. *1835 -* Towns were allowed to set up their own police force.
3. *1839 -* Allowed counties to set up their own police force. *Bow street runners* and other forces merged into the met police.
4. *1842 -* Metropolitan police set up their first detective branch.
5. *1856 -* **Compulsory** for all towns and counites to have their own force.
6. *1884 -* There were now 39,000 police over 200 separate forces.

# Bloody Code
## Introduction
***Between 1668 and 1815 crimes punishable by death rose to 225***. *This included poaching and theft*
## Abolition of Bloody Code 1820-30
### Reasons
1. MPs worried that large crowds might become rebellious and start a riot.
2. Juries becoming increasing reluctant to convict as they felt it was unfair.
3. Transportation had been used since the 1780s and seen as a better punishment  - more fair and juries mor likely to convict.
4. Crowds at executions were growing - dangerous.
5. *Sir Robert Peel's* campaigning.
6. Ineffective - crime rate was decreasing before bloody code then increased after it's introduction.
### Key Events
1. *1789 -* Last women burned. 
2. *1808 -* Pickpocketing death penalty abolished.
3. *1820 -* Last beheading 
4. *1841 -* Only murder and treason remained as having death penalties.
5. *1861 -* Last public haninging.
