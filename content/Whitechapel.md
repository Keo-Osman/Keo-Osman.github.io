# Working Conditions
## General Ideas
1. Very long hours.
2. Low pay.
3. Poor conditions during work.
## Specific Jobs
### Tailor/Shoemaker (Sweated Trades)
1. Called sweated trades as they work in small rooms that were very hot and had little natural light.
2. The room is rarely cleaned and it has a large number of fleas.
3. 16+hr days with 1p/hr pay.
4. Fined for being late making goods.
### Iron Foundry
1. Work with heavy machinery and furnaces: very dangerous.
2. 10hr/day with low pay.
3. Very hot and tiring.
4. One foundry had a furnace explode killing 14 workers.
### Prostitute
1. Charge 1p.
2. In dark backstreets. Vulnerable and often attacked or murdered.
3. Get business from men drinking at pub.
4. Often arrested for other crimes.

# Social Tensions
## Criminal Underclass
1. People believed there was a class of people who were *"natural criminals"* (sometimes called "residuum" - literally meaning the *dregs of humanity*).
2. People believed that the residuum were attracted to hard-working people and lived off of them.
3. This worsened people's view of Whitechapel.
## Lodging Houses and Pubs
1. People believed the bad living conditions and overcrowding led to criminal behaviour so pubs and lodging houses were often seen as places of crime.
## Drinking
1. Drinking was seen as a cause for many crimes.
2. Alcohol made people more likely to be victims of crime or commit a crime as they might start or make arguments worse leading to fights.
3. People drank in Whitechapel because of:
	1. Depression.
	2. Poverty.
	3. Addiction.
	4. Cheap and abundant.
## Prostitution
1. Many women had to turn to prostitution for a living.
2. Brothels were made illegal in 1885. This made people view prostitution as a more serious crime.
3. Many saw it as a social problem rather than a criminal problem.
## Immigration
### Irish
1. First arrived in the 1840s. Many were young men and often drunk and violent.
2. Many Irish nationalists wanted freedom from the UK.
3. There was a group called *The Fenians* who were a Catholic group. They were seen as wild/extravagant and extreme in their religious views: a terrorist movement.
	1. in 1867 they organised a bomb attack on a prison. Police ignored warnings of the attack.
	2. In 1885 on *Dynamite Saturday* they launched simultaneous attacks on central London. A year after the *Special Irish Branch* was set up to infiltrate Irish terrorist organisations.
### Jewish
1. 1st arrived in 1880s. They fled from Russia.
2. In some areas of Whitechapel the population was 95% Jewish.
3. They caused tensions as:
	1. Many set up businesses that competed with locals. Many used sweatshops meaning businesses that followed guidelines could not compete.
	2. Some people preferred employing arrivals over locals so wages went down.
	3. They easily stood out.
	4. Competing for housing.
## Anarchists
From the middle of the 19th century, social and economic problems across Europe caused the growth of many revolutionary movements that thought the answer was to overthrow the government.
1. In 1871 revolutionists took control of Paris.
2. Many people started to see East End as a place of refuge for other nations terrorists.
3. In 1893 the special branch monitored the activities of Eastern European revolutionaries.
4. Anyone with an Eastern European accent was seen as a potential anarchist.
## Socialist
1. The *SDF* (Social Democratic Federation) were the first socialist party. They were formed to represent agricultural and industrial labourers.
2. The radical leadership wanted a revolution to take down the capitalist system.
3. They were involved in the Trafalgar Square demonstration.
4. The SDF viewed the police as a face of the government that cared for nothing.
5. Socialists though Whitechapel was a promising area to get an SDF councillor as the could highlight the stupidity of the police with the Jack the Ripper case.

# Police
*Links* [[Industrial Period Crime and Punishment#Metropolitan Police Force 1829|Metropolitan Police]]
## Overview of Police
1. *1888* - There was 5m people with 14k policemen ~ 1 policemen per 390 people.
2. 31% of recruits were from the countryside and 12% were ex-soldiers. Recruits from the countryside were better as they were bigger and healthier with better character.
3. Recruits were expected to be disciplined, follow orders, truthful, able to control their temper and literate.
4. In 1870s there were 82 laws expected to be enforced.
5. Police would deal with:
	1. Homelessness.
	2. Drunkenness.
	3. Murder.
	4. Lost children.
	5. Lunatics.
6. Many police officers ran soup kitchens.
7. *June 1886 London Dog Hunt* - An loose/out of control dog would be seized by police.
### The Beat
1. The Beat Constable's role was to prevent crime by being an obvious presence.
2. The beat was a patrol that each beat constable had to do. It was very specifically timed so different beat constables could communicate with each other.
3. They were shorter at night as there was more crime at night and they wanted to give burglars less time.
4. Criminals could work out the routine and plan around it.
## Attitudes
1. *1835* - Newspapers praised the quick response to the Millbank prison fire.
2. *1842* - Detective branch setup and many were worried that they would be used to spy on ordinary people.
3. *1866* - 3200 policemen were used to control a riot in Hyde Park and the army had to be called in.
4. *1877* - Court case revealed that there was corruption with senior detectives in an international gambling fraud conspiracy.
5. *1889* - Police code introduce. A set of instructions on how police were supposed to behave.
### Bloody Sunday - Trafalgar Square (1887)
There was a protest due to high unemployment and poverty.
1. 2 people died.
2. 1004 people injured.
3. 40 arrests made.
4. 75 charges of police brutality.

## Commissioners
### Edmund Henderson (1870-1886)
1. Adventurer and Soldier.
2. Allowed police to vote.
3. Set up a charity for widows and orphans of police who died in service.
4. Created a register of habitual criminals.
5. Reduced military drills.
#### Reason for Resignation
1. *1887* - CID gambling bribery scandal.
2. *1880* - Thomas Titley. Police accused of pressuring him to commit a crime.
3. *1884/85* - Series of bombs set off and police had no clue.
4. *1886* - Riot in Trafalgar Square.
### Sir Charles Warren (1886-1888)
1. Ex-Army.
2. Found and killed a group of English criminals.
3. Increased military drills.
4. Brought more ex-soldiers into the force.
#### Reason for Resignation
1. *1887* - Bloody Sunday riot.
2. *1888* - Jack the Ripper murders.
3. Tension with home secretary - He didn't get his article defending police actions approved.

## Challenges Policing Whitechapel
1. Environment
	1. Criminals could easily hide in rookeries as they were very crowded
2. Alcohol and Pubs
	1. Makes people more vulnerable to crime.
	2. After 1870 it became illegal to serve alcohol to somebody who was drunk. This reduced the chance of somebody becoming incapacitated.

# Jack the Ripper (1888)
## Police Methods Used
1. Careful Observation.
	1. Crime scenes were to preserved and carefully checked by an inspector.
2. Photography and Sketches.
	1. Often depicted as Jewish locals believed no Englishmen could such crimes.
3. Autopsy.
	1. Used thermometer to tell how long ago victim was killed.
	2. Knew they were looking for a left handed murderer with good surgical skills.
4. Interviews.
	1. Went to houses and business in the area of the murders.
	2. Interview 3000 people.
5. Identification.
	1. Take notes from witnesses to build a profile.
	2. Identification parade - line up people to see if there is a match.
## Difficulties
1. Rivalry between divisions - lots of information kept secret.
2. Lack of technology.
3. Whitechapel Vigilance Comity - interfered with investigations.
4. Sensationalised news stories - worsened police reputations so people were less willing to cooperate.
5. Many fake Ripper letters sent in.
