#English/Macbeth 
# Hamartia of Ambition  
Macbeth’s hamartia (tragic flaw) is his ambition. He desires to be king: the highest order a human can be. In doing so he is directly defying the natural order as well as defying God’s orders in the form of Duncan’s divine right as king. 

The witches feed this ambition by giving him a prophecy, blinded by his ambition fueled by a belief in the supernatural’s prophecy he commits the heinous crime of regicide. “Macbeth’s blindness to the meaning of the witches’ prophecies convinces him he is invincible”. This belief eventually leads to his ultimate downfall
### Quotes  
1. "I have no spur to prick the sides of my intent, but only vaulting ambition." – said by Macbeth when he acknowledges that ambition alone motivates him to kill Duncan.  
2. "Stars, hide your fires; let not light see my black and deep desires." – said by Macbeth when he reveals his secret, ambitious thoughts about becoming king.  
3. "To be thus is nothing, but to be safely thus." – said by Macbeth when he realises that his ambition requires him to continue killing to secure his throne.

# Chaos and Disorder  
Macbeth’s regicide against Duncan tears apart the natural and social order, leading to widespread chaos. Nature itself reacts violently to the unnatural act, and Macbeth’s personal stability disintegrates. As Macbeth descends into tyranny, Scotland suffers under his rule, showing the collapse of both internal and societal order.

### Quotes  
1. "By th' clock 'tis day, and yet dark night strangles the travelling lamp." – said by Ross when he describes unnatural darkness following Duncan’s murder.  
2. "O, full of scorpions is my mind, dear wife!" – said by Macbeth when he reveals the internal chaos consuming him.  
3. "Each new morn new widows howl, new orphans cry, new sorrows strike heaven on the face." – said by Macduff when he describes the suffering of Scotland under Macbeth’s tyranny.

---
# Inevitability  
Once Macbeth commits to murder, his downfall becomes inevitable as he sinks deeper into violence and paranoia. Despite attempts to control his fate, he is trapped by the witches’ prophecies and his own decisions. His death is both unavoidable and a necessary restoration of natural order.

### Quotes  
1. "If chance will have me king, why, chance may crown me without my stir." – said by Macbeth when he briefly considers letting destiny unfold naturally.  
2. "From this moment the very firstlings of my heart shall be the firstlings of my hand." – said by Macbeth when he resolves to act without hesitation, sealing his fate.  
3. "I 'gin to be aweary of the sun, and wish th' estate o' th' world were now undone." – said by Macbeth when he expresses despair and acceptance of death in the final act.

---
# Tragic Hero  
Macbeth fits the Aristotelian tragic hero model: he is noble and brave but ruined by ambition, external influences, and fate. Although he commits terrible deeds, he retains a certain tragic dignity through his fearless end. His death restores the broken moral and social order.

### Quotes  
1. "For brave Macbeth—well he deserves that name." – said by Captain when praising Macbeth’s heroism at the start.  
2. "Is this a dagger which I see before me, the handle toward my hand?" – said by Macbeth when he hallucinates, torn by ambition and conscience.  
3. "Lay on, Macduff, and damn'd be him that first cries, 'Hold, enough!'" – said by Macbeth when he faces death bravely in his final battle.
