#English/Macbeth
### Short Summary
 After victorious battles, ***Macbeth*** encounters three ***Witches*** who prophesy he will become king, igniting his ruthless ambition. Encouraged ***Lady Macbeth***, he *murders* **King Duncan**. Consumed by paranoia, ***Macbeth*** orders the assassination of his friend ***Banquo*** *(whose descendants the witches promised would rule)* and Banquo’s son Fleance, who escapes. 
 
When ***Macbeth*** seeks the ***Witches*** again, they mislead him with cryptic assurances: he *cannot be harmed by any man “born of woman”* and will *remain safe until Birnam Wood marches*. Meanwhile, ***Lady Macbeth*** unravels with guilt, sleepwalking and obsessively trying to wash imaginary blood from her hands. 

***Macbeth***’s cruelty escalates as he slaughters ***Macduff***’s family, prompting ***Macduff*** to join forces with Duncan’s exiled son, ***Malcolm***, to overthrow the tyrant. As ***Malcolm***’s army advances, soldiers disguise themselves with branches from *Birnam Wood*, fulfilling the prophecy.
In the final battle, ***Macduff*** reveals he was *“untimely ripped” from his mother’s womb (not “born of woman”)* and **kills** ***Macbeth***, restoring *rightful rule* to ***Malcolm***.

### Act I 
1. The three witches gather in a thunderstorm to meet Macbeth.
2. Duncan hears reports of the battle in which Macbeth proves himself a hero and also of the treachery of the Thane of Cawdor.
3. Macbeth & Banquo meet the witches and hear the predictions that he will be Thane of Cawdor and the next king. Ross arrives to confirm that Macbeth is the new Thane of Cawdor. 
4. Duncan decides to make his son Malcolm the heir to his throne and tells Macbeth that he will visit his castle. 
5. Lady Macbeth reads a letter from her husband about the events so far and makes up her mind to murder Duncan. 
6. Duncan arrives at Macbeth’s castle and is welcomed by Lady Macbeth. 
7. Macbeth decides he cannot go through with the plot but Lady Macbeth persuades him to change his mind.
### Act II
1. Banquo feels uneasy about what might happen in the night. Macbeth makes his way to Duncan’s room to kill him and sees a ghostly dagger floating in the air before him. 
2. Macbeth forgets to leave the bloody daggers in Duncan’s room after the murder and Lady Macbeth is forced to take charge and put them back. 
3. The next morning Duncan’s body is discovered by Macduff; Macbeth conveniently kills the servants in pretend rage; Duncan’s sons, Malcolm & Donalbain, flee the castle. 
4. Macduff reports that suspicion for the murder has fallen on the king’s sons; Macbeth has travelled to Scone to be crowned.
### Act III 
1. Macbeth is now king, but Banquo is suspicious about how the witches’ predictions have come true. Macbeth arranges to have him murdered. 
2. Lady Macbeth tries to get her husband to talk to her about his plans but he refuses. 
3. Banquo is murdered but his son, Fleance, escapes.
4. At a feast that night, Macbeth sees the ghost of Banquo. Lady Macbeth tries to calm him down but when this fails cancels the feasts and sends the courtiers away. 
5. The witches discuss events so far; Hecate, the ruler of the witches, predicts his downfall.
6. suspicion of Macbeth is growing; Macduff has left for England to rouse support against him.
### Act IV 
1. The witches tell Macbeth he cannot be harmed by anyone ‘born of a woman’ and that he will be safe until Birnam Wood moves to the castle at Dunsinane. Macbeth decides to murder Macduff’s family.
2. Macbeth’s murderers kill Lady Macduff and her children. 
3. Macduff discovers his family’s murder and, with Malcolm, leads an army to attack Macbeth.
### Act V
1. Lady Macbeth is sleepwalking and trying to wash an imaginary blood spot from her hands.
2. Malcolm’s army is at Birnam Wood and hear reports that Macbeth’s supporters are deserting him. 
3. Macbeth is besieged but puts his trust in the witches’ prophesy. 
4. Malcolm orders his army to cut down branches from Birnam Wood to disguise the number of soldiers. 
5. Macbeth is told of his wife’s death and about the news that Birnam Wood seems to be approaching. He resolves to die fighting. 
6. (scene vi – ix) Macbeth is killed by Macduff (who reveals he was delivered by caesarean and so not properly ‘born’). Malcolm becomes the new king of Scotland and order is restored.
